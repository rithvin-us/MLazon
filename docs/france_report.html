France test-set diagnosis

Why the one country with no answer key scores about 0.92 while the US and India score about 0.986, what exactly is different about it, and the fixes planned for the next submissions.

Verdict

  • France is the gap. The leaderboard score 0.975411 splits into roughly 0.986 on US/India and about 0.916 on France. France alone costs about 0.0105 of leaderboard score; the whole distance to the top (0.9906) is 0.0152.
  • The main problem is crowding. French records are built from a tiny vocabulary: about 15 cities, 15,000 distinct address words (US 109,000, India 302,000) and generic names like Club, Ecole, Amicale + SARL/SAS. Many different businesses look almost identical, in the name and the address at the same time.
  • The model never practised on that. It was trained on US/India, where either the name (US) or the address (India) is usually distinctive. On France it is unsure 3–4× more often and over-confident: France needs a threshold of 0.895 where US/India use 0.625.
  • Cleaning is not broken. One small gap (French départements) and a few rare formats; none of them explains the gap by itself.
  • Fix direction: build a France-like practice test from US/India data so France work can be measured, then retrain with more weight on crowded cases, add street-identity features and clean départements.

1. Why France cannot be scored at home

Think of the competition as a school exam.

Practice papers, with answers

The training data covers only the US and India and comes with the answer key. We hide part of it from the model, let it answer, and check. That check is our 0.986.

The real exam, no answers

The test data has US, India and France. Amazon keeps the answer key. There is no French practice paper anywhere in the training data.

Only a total mark

The leaderboard returns one number for the whole paper, never a mark per country. France is 15% of it, and each look costs one of five daily submissions.

So a France improvement of 0.02 shows up as only 0.003 on the leaderboard, mixed with whatever changed on US/India. The implied France score has an error bar of about ±0.006, because an error of 0.001 on US/India moves it by that much.

What we can still do without answers: check that France gets the same number of matches per business as the US and India (it should, the data generator is the same), watch how often the model is unsure, read small samples by eye, and build a France-like stand-in test from labelled US/India data (fix F1).

How the copies are made (inferred from samples and counts)

Each French business has on average 3.36 copies in Source 2/3, exactly as in the US and India. The noise applied to copies is the same family as in training, with a few French variants:

  • Case and accents: BABOUK CLUB EURL, Communale Spôrtive, Çlub; 24% of French copies contain non-ASCII letters.
  • Abbreviations: R. / R for rue, AV for avenue, E.U.R.L., SASU, Établissements for Ets.
  • Look-alike digits: Nationa1e, mas0n.
  • Extra or dropped words: Groupe, & Fils, France, dropped legal form.
  • Aliases: handles (@nantesecole), domains (Ehpadmarie.Com), trading-as (Arczephx t/a Communale Sportive SAS).
  • Address: parts reordered or number dropped (15% do not start with the house number), No. 13 Rue … (5.7%), 12 bis / ter (4.0%, French only), address dropped (3%), département instead of region (32%).

3. The main problem compared with the US and India

India already has crowded names: half its businesses share a core name with another business. But Indian addresses are long and varied (12 words, 302,000 distinct words), so the address still tells them apart. The US has distinctive names. France is the only country where both name and address are crowded at once, and the training data never contains that combination.

5. What was checked and works

Cleaning

  • Accents and Ç removed; R., AV, BD → rue, ave, blvd
  • E.U.R.L. → eurl, SASU → sas, Établissements → ets
  • Look-alike digits: Nationa1e → nationale
  • French regions → codes (hdf, naq, pdl), stop words de/la/le/du/des/et
  • No rule anywhere in the code is specific to one country

Decision and ownership

  • Matches per business in France follow the US/India distribution: 5.3% none, 17.8% two, 24.1% three, 21.3% four, 13.8% five
  • Same-name same-number different-street pairs: only 3.5% given to the S1, 72% given to another S1
  • Same name, number and street: mean p 0.963, 95.6% assigned
  • No postcodes in French data (0.4%), same as India, so nothing is lost there

7. Fixes, in the order to run them

Expected gains are estimates. France cannot be measured directly, so F1 comes first: it turns the rest from guesses into measurements.

F1France-like stand-in test

measurement · 30 min CPU
What
From the labelled US/India validation set, select businesses as crowded as France: 15 or more candidates, or a same-name same-number look-alike. Report F0.5 on that slice for every model.
Why
It gives a France proxy score that needs no submission. Every later fix is kept only if this slice improves.
Gain
None on its own; required to judge F2–F6.

F2Crowding-weighted retrain

model · ~2 h GPU
What
Retrain the matcher on the saved training features with 3× weight on crowded training businesses (the retrain command already supports row weights), then re-score test and re-apply the cross-encoder.
Why
The model hesitates on France because crowded cases are rare in training. More weight on the India cases that look like France teaches it which small differences matter.
Gain
France +0.01 to +0.03, which is leaderboard +0.0015 to +0.0045.
Risk
US/India may drop slightly; rejected if validation falls by more than 0.0003.

F3Département → region cleaning

cleaning · ~2 h CPU
What
Map département names to their region code, but only when a whole comma-separated part of the address is a département name, so US/India addresses are untouched. Rebuild the test features and cross-encoder scores; no retraining needed.
Why
32% of French copies name the département while S1 names the region, the same kind of variant as "Maryland" vs "MD" that is already handled for the US.
Gain
Leaderboard +0.000 to +0.002. Low risk.

F4Street-identity features

features · ~5 h, day 2
What
New features comparing only the street name (without house number, street type, city, region or département), weighted by how rare the street is within the country, plus whether the rest of the address after the house number matches.
Why
The French look-alikes differ mainly in the street or in one generic word. Current address features are diluted by the city and region words that all French addresses share.
Gain
France +0.01 to +0.03. Needs a full feature rebuild for training, so it is the largest job.

F5Legal-form swap check

analysis · 15 min
What
Measure in the training answers how often true matches change legal form (LLC ↔ Inc, Pvt Ltd ↔ Ltd) compared with non-matches.
Why
4.9% of uncertain French pairs have a SARL/SAS swap. If swaps are mostly noise in true matches, the legal-conflict feature should not push these pairs down.
Gain
Unknown until measured.

F6Smaller candidate lists for crowded businesses

candidates · 1 h
What
Tighten the candidate keep rule where lists are long; keep only if validation recall holds.
Why
France is 15% of businesses but 5.6 million of the 19.5 million candidate pairs. A smaller candidate file ranks higher in the final judging.
Gain
Smaller candidate_pairs.tsv; neutral on F0.5 if recall is kept.

F7One diagnostic submission

1 of 5 daily submissions
What
Submit v9_plain, which differs from the submitted file only in the France threshold (3.44 instead of 3.36 matches per business).
Why
The leaderboard difference divided by 0.15 is a direct France measurement, the only one available.

Already tried

  • France-adapted cross-encoder (v9_cefr): validation 0.98531 against 0.98585, not submitted.
  • Self-training on French pseudo-labels: +0.004 on the US→India stand-in, no change on the leaderboard.
  • Matching France's matches per business to US/India (threshold 0.895): part of the +0.0015 gain in v9_ce.

9. Overnight results (27 Sep)

What to upload

  • output/SUBMIT_THIS/matching_results.tsv: US/India rows from v10, France rows from v10s, both with the test-density decision. Validator with --check-ids: PASS.
  • Expected leaderboard about 0.976 (range 0.975–0.978), up from 0.975411. 0.985 is out of reach with tonight's changes; see below.

Finding 1: test is denser than validation

  • Test has 5.8 Source 2/3 records per business against 4.7 in training, with the same ~3.46 true matches.
  • Uncertain pairs per business are about twice validation's (US 0.32 vs 0.16).
  • On validation made equally dense, v9 + cross-encoder scores 0.9841, not 0.9859. So US/India on test is about 0.984 and France about 0.93.
  • A new redecide step re-tunes the threshold for this density.

Finding 2: country-IDF features

  • Words weighted by how rare they are in their own country. Stage-1 validation rose from 0.9832 to 0.9850 (v10).
  • The unseen-country check (train on one country, score the other): address-IDF features hurt transfer (−0.009), name-IDF features help (+0.0024).
  • So France uses v10s (name-IDF only) and US/India use v10 (all IDF features).
